Microgravity Blood Infusion Pump

Space Biomedical Systems, Purdue Aerospace Medicine VIP. Founding operations director and medical instruments EE co-lead, Aug 2025 – present.

On Earth, an IV bag relies on gravity to push blood and float air bubbles out of the line. In microgravity neither happens, so a single stray bubble can reach the patient. We designed a gravity-independent transfusion system for reconstituted red blood cells that fits in an ISS locker.

A peristaltic pump drives the flow. Air is pulled out through a vacuum-backed ePTFE membrane bubble trap, and an ultrasonic air-in-line sensor stops the pump if anything gets past. I designed the 28 V ISS rack power distribution — fuse, TVS and bulk-capacitor protection, buck-regulated 9 V and 5 V rails, and a MOSFET driver with flyback protection for the vacuum pump. We presented it to NASA engineers at Marshall Space Flight Center.

Wheelchair-integrated rehab exoskeleton

Kennesaw State University Mechatronics. Undergraduate researcher, Aug 2024 – Aug 2026, with Dr. Razvan Voicu.

People who use wheelchairs lose lower-limb muscle when their legs don't move. Rehab usually means transferring the patient to a separate machine. This 2-DOF knee and ankle exoskeleton mounts to the wheelchair and runs off its battery, so therapy can happen in the chair.

I designed the modular power and mounting system, motor mounts for CubeMars AK10-9 actuators, and motor control on a Raspberry Pi over CAN. We verified the controller on an encoder-instrumented rig with coupled sinusoidal knee and ankle trials. The work is published at IEEE SoutheastCon 2025.

Tremor suppression glove In progress

Purdue MIND. Lead mechanical engineer, Aug 2026 – present.

A glove to restore hand function for people with pathological tremor, built in three tiers: IMU tracking, sEMG co-contraction detection, and mechanical damping. I'm leading the mechanical design and a custom KiCad PCB that replaces the prototype's breadboard wiring.

A small mass on a spring, tuned to the tremor frequency, absorbs the oscillation passively.

About me

A bit about me

From Woodstock, Georgia to West Lafayette, Indiana.

I like problems where the engineering has to account for a person. A transfusion pump is only useful if it never sends air to the patient; an exoskeleton is only useful if someone can use it without leaving their chair.

Most of my work sits between mechanical design and embedded systems: CAD and machining on one side, power electronics, sensors and control code on the other. I started on the exoskeleton as a high school intern at Kennesaw State and kept going.

I'm looking for research and internship opportunities in medical devices, aerospace and robotics.
